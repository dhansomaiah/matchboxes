How it works

There is no strategy in the code. A win puts three more beads behind every move it made, and a draw puts one. When it loses, the move that let you win is thrown out of its box for good, so it never loses the same way twice.

It also remembers the squares you chose. Between games it plays a thousand practice games in its head against that memory of you, which is why it improves after a handful of games and not a few hundred.

Donald Michie built the original in 1961 out of 304 real matchboxes, because he had no computer to hand. Everything here is kept in your browser, so it only ever learns from you.
